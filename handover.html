The journey

How the site evolved

From a single landing page in July to a 21-page illustrated beta in September.

  1. 14 Jul 2026

    First landing page

    A single GoSports landing page and a README describing the project.

  2. 31 Jul 2026

    The full beta build

    The complete site was built from two sources: the vendor's content and wireframe document, and GoSports' FY 2025–26 Annual Report. The homepage became a six-part story (Who we are → What we do → How we do it → Who's funded us → What's next → Come work for us). A "Find your lane" section routes the seven audiences: academies, CSR professionals, athletes, ecosystem partners, philanthropists, job applicants and researchers. New pages were added for all 12 programmes, Our Funders, the SAPA Centre and the Performance Institute. Search-engine indexing was blocked.

  3. 11 Sep 2026

    Hosting, brand and photography

    • Automatic deploys: pushing to GitHub publishes to Hostinger, with the server locked down so internal files are never served.
    • Photography: 18 of 28 empty image slots filled with photos from the Annual Report. Athletes are only named where the report ties the photo to them.
    • Mobile menu fixed: it was broken on every page, leaving most of the site unreachable on a phone.
    • Brand styleguide applied: the live gosports.in red, dark, white and Satoshi typeface, plus the brand's rounded buttons.
    • Gallery and asset library: reusable images imported from gosports.in, optimised, and catalogued on a new Gallery page. Trustee and funder images were upgraded.
  4. 12 Sep 2026

    Real logo, full UI/UX audit, fixes

    • The real GoSports logo and photography went onto the homepage.
    • A full audit of all 21 pages at phone and desktop sizes, using automated checks plus a page-by-page visual review. About 45 issues were found. The layout, image and accessibility ones were fixed the same day. The content decisions are listed below.
    • Among the fixes:
      • Testimonials on programme pages had been squeezed into a thin column.
      • Two programme logos showed as black boxes.
      • The Gallery's filter bar was hidden under the header.
      • Placeholder "PHOTO" boxes were replaced with real leadership headshots.
      • Partner logos now sit at similar sizes.
      • The mobile menu has a close button and the Partner call to action.
      • Some text had too little contrast to read comfortably.
    • Phones were holding on to old copies of the design, so updates now reach them straight away.

Before public launch

Launch checklist

The beta deliberately shows reviewers some things a public visitor should never see. Remove all of these before launch.

  • Remove the "Beta note" boxes and editorial asides. They're currently on:
    • Home: "What's next" section
    • About: leadership titles, and the research hub
    • Get Involved: donation amounts
    • Programmes: source line
    • Our Funders: logo files, "keep in sync", "Sustaining the System" recommendation, "built to grow"
    • SAPA Centre
    • The Gallery page's audit notes
  • Remove "Beta site — content in review." from the footer of every page.
  • Remove or finish every item in Still to do. No placeholder boxes should remain.
  • Get sign-off on everything in Waiting for review, especially the Performance Institute page.
  • Switch search engines back on. Three separate blocks must all come out: robots.txt, the noindex tag at the top of every page, and a server setting. Your developer will know them from DEPLOY.md.
  • Until launch, switch the password back on before sharing the beta link beyond the core team.
  • Decide the Gallery's future. It's an internal asset catalogue that's currently in the main menu. Remove it from the menu, or rewrite it for the public.
Working on the site

How to make changes safely

Editing text

Each page is one .html file in the repository, named after the page (see the site map). Edit the words between the tags and commit to main. The change is live in about 40 seconds. You can edit directly on GitHub in the browser: open the file, click the pencil icon, then commit.

Adding or replacing a photo

Put new images in assets/img/gallery/. Always give a replacement photo a new file name rather than overwriting the old one, because browsers keep old images for a week. Resize to about 1600px wide before uploading.

Naming people in photos

Only name an athlete in a photo's description when the source clearly shows it's them. Otherwise describe the scene, and mark the photo as representative with the same REPRESENTATIVE IMAGE comment used on the programme pages.

If a change doesn't show up

Reload the page with the browser's reload button. On a phone, returning to an open tab can show an old copy. If you or a developer change the stylesheet (styles.css) or the script (main.js), bump the ?v= number where every page links to them. This step is covered in DEPLOY.md.

Check on a phone

Most visitors will be on a phone. After any layout change, look at the page on a phone or a narrow browser window, and open the menu once.

Deeper reference

README.md in the repository has the full reasoning behind the content and design choices, plus every image's source. DEPLOY.md covers hosting, caching and security. Neither file is published on the site.
